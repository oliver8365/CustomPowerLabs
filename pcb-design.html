How we work

Layout that respects the physics.

Most board problems are not in the schematic. They are in the return paths, the power delivery and the gaps between them. We plan the stackup, the reference planes and the decoupling before routing starts, so fast signals always have a clean path home and the power rails stay quiet under load.

We design in Altium Designer. Boards go to established board houses for speed and cost, or to UK-based fabrication and assembly when your IP, export rules or supply chain make that the better choice.

Electronics is rarely the whole product, so we lay out the board with the enclosure, connectors, cooling and firmware in view. The same team designs all of them.

Questions

Common questions.

How complex a board can you design?

Our work includes dense multilayer boards with DDR memory and high-speed camera, display and Ethernet interfaces. We choose the layer count to suit the design rather than the other way round, and we will recommend the most economical stackup that meets your requirements.

Can you take over or review an existing design?

Yes. We can review a design before it goes to fabrication, take over an existing Altium project, or redraw a legacy board. Altium imports most other formats, including KiCad and Eagle.

Where are the boards made?

We use established board houses for quick, low-cost prototypes. When IP, export rules or the supply chain matter, we use UK-based fabrication and assembly instead.

What do we receive at the end?

The Altium project, a schematic PDF, Gerber or ODB++ files, drill files, a bill of materials, pick-and-place data and assembly drawings. If we built the prototype too, you get the assembled boards.

Will you sign an NDA?

Yes. We are happy to sign a non-disclosure agreement before you share the details of your product.

How we work

Layout, heat and EMC come first.

In power electronics the layout is part of the circuit. We keep high-current switching loops tight, choose magnetics and switching frequency together, and plan the path heat takes out of the product before routing starts. That is what keeps a converter efficient, cool and quiet enough to pass EMC testing.

Motor drives need the hardware and the control code designed together. We write the field-oriented control firmware ourselves, so the current sensing, PWM timing and protection all match the board they run on.

Where it helps, we simulate before we build: electromagnetic simulation for motors and magnetics, and thermal checks on the hot parts.

Questions

Common questions.

What power levels do you work at?

Our work spans point-of-load regulators and battery-powered supplies through to mains-input converters and motor drives. The engineering scales with power, with closer attention to thermal design, insulation and protection, so tell us what your product needs.

Can you write the motor control firmware too?

Yes. We design the drive hardware and write the control firmware, including field-oriented control, sensorless start-up and CAN communication. See embedded firmware.

Sensored or sensorless?

Sensorless control saves the cost and wiring of position sensors and works well once the motor is turning. Applications that need full torque from standstill or precise low-speed positioning usually need Hall sensors or an encoder. We will recommend one for your load.

Do you design to safety and EMC standards?

We design to the isolation, creepage and clearance requirements of the relevant standard, and with EMC testing in mind. Formal certification is carried out by an accredited lab.
